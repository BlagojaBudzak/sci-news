# Sci News — Seismology — 2026-09-01

## Uncovering a Hidden Pattern in Earthquakes

Researchers have discovered a fascinating phenomenon in earthquakes: 'anti-similar' events, where two earthquakes have nearly identical but flipped waveforms. They found three locations where these events occur at intermediate depth: Colombia, Alaska, and Romania. This new evidence suggests that anti-similar earthquake sequences might be a common process of intermediate-depth seismicity. The study proposes two possible explanations for their occurrence: conjugate faulting promoted by eclogitization-induced stresses or sub-parallel faulting.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26448v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26448v1)

## Lab Experiments Confirm Theory of Frictional Slip Pulses

Scientists have conducted extensive lab earthquake experiments to test a theory of frictional slip pulses. The theory predicts that pulses are unstable rupture modes with intrinsically slow unsteady dynamics. The experiments confirmed the theoretical predictions for pulse-like lab earthquakes over a range of prestress levels, rupture nucleation conditions, and small-scale fault roughness amplitudes. The study demonstrated the predicted time-dependent dynamics and the transition between decaying and growing pulses.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26065v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26065v1)

## New AI Model Improves Seismic Data Analysis

Researchers have developed a new AI model called GeoFormer, specifically designed for prestack seismic data. GeoFormer incorporates acquisition geometry and performs trace-level tokenization, combining waveforms with geometric attributes. The model introduces three geometry injection mechanisms to exploit these attributes. The study validated GeoFormer on first-arrival picking, a representative seismic processing task, and demonstrated its potential for improving seismic data analysis.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25668v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25668v1)

## New Approach Simplifies Waveform Inversion

Scientists have introduced a new approach called ROMNet, which uses a neural network to map a reduced order model (ROM) matrix to a nearby one with a simpler dependence on wave speed. This simplifies and reduces the computational cost of ROM-based waveform inversion. The study tested ROMNet with numerical simulations and demonstrated its potential for improving waveform inversion.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25160v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25160v1)

## Revisiting the Cosmic Shoreline

Researchers have re-examined the traditional 'cosmic shoreline' using a coupled atmosphere-interior evolution model. They found that atmosphere retention is governed by two boundaries: a hot, outgassing-regulated 'cosmic sandbar' and a cooler, escape-regulated 'cosmic shoreline.' The study provided time-evolving fits for both boundaries across different stellar types and planetary characteristics.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23912v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23912v1)
