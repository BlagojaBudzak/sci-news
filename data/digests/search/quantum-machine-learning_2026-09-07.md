# Sci News — Quantum-Machine-Learning — 2026-09-07

## Quantum Hamiltonian Evolution for Coherent Quantum Learning

Researchers have introduced a new training framework for quantum learning models called Coherent Quantum Learning (CQL). In CQL, the model parameters are treated as quantum degrees of freedom that evolve under a Hamiltonian that encodes the loss function. This approach allows the model to learn without the need for classical optimization or gradient computation. The researchers demonstrate the effectiveness of CQL on binary classification and interferometric phase estimation tasks, showing that the evolved distribution peaks at the optimal parameters, matching gradient-based performance.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03640v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03640v1)

## A Unified Physics-Aware Quantum Machine Learning Framework across Power GaN HEMTs and Logic Nanowire FETs

Researchers have developed a unified reinforcement-learning framework that discovers compact parametrized quantum circuits for data-scarce device modeling. The framework uses a graph neural network policy optimized by proximal policy optimization to search circuit architectures. The researchers demonstrate the effectiveness of the framework on two device datasets, achieving the lowest mean absolute error compared to six classical baselines.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05251v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05251v1)

## SAR and InSAR Change Detection with Quantum Generative Models

Researchers have integrated state-of-the-art satellite imagery with quantum machine learning on IonQ trapped-ion-based quantum processors. They replaced the empirical conditional with a quantum circuit Born machine (QCBM)-sampled generative model in Copula space, improving change detection on sparse real-world images. The researchers demonstrate the effectiveness of the generative estimator on Capella Space satellite image acquisitions, showing that it matches conventional methods when the observed statistics are adequate and substantially outperforms them when they are not.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05313v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05313v1)

## Discretization-Aware Fine-Tuning for Quantum Machine Learning with Chemical Foundation Models

Researchers have introduced discretization-aware fine-tuning (DAFT), a method that adapts a pre-trained chemical foundation model to produce representations that remain informative after quantization. DAFT reduces collision probability through a differentiable soft collision loss. The researchers demonstrate the effectiveness of DAFT on the blood-brain barrier penetration (BBBP) molecular property prediction benchmark, showing that it reduces collision counts by several orders of magnitude and improves quantum classification accuracy by more than 12 percentage points compared to a frozen backbone.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03220v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03220v1)

## Impact of Data Loss in Postprocessing on Training and Inference of Quantum Neural Networks

Researchers have investigated the impact of data loss in postprocessing on the training and inference of quantum neural networks. They found that the postprocessing method applies a filter that assumes measurement bit-strings are in virtual qubit space, leading to the loss of 85 to 99.6% of valid measurement shots on quantum hardware runs. The resulting probability vector is unnormalised, allowing distorted prediction and loss values to propagate through the model without an API-level warning.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05060v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05060v1)
