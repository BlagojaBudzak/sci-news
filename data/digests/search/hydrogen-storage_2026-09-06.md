# Sci News — Hydrogen-Storage — 2026-09-06

## Unlocking the Secrets of B12C12 Nanocages for Hydrogen Storage

Researchers used density functional theory to investigate the hydrogen storage properties of pristine and metal-decorated B12C12 nanocages. They found that substituting certain metals, such as titanium and scandium, into the nanocages significantly improved their ability to store hydrogen. However, the titanium-decorated nanocages showed near-covalent chemisorption, which limited their suitability for reversible hydrogen release. The study provides valuable insights into the design of efficient hydrogen storage materials.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67351-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67351-x)

## Building a Unified Framework for Solid-State Hydrogen Storage Materials

A team of researchers proposed a unified framework that integrates data infrastructure, physics-grounded modeling, and AI-driven inverse design for solid-state hydrogen storage materials. This framework aims to overcome the limitations of current approaches by constraining optimization with thermodynamics, kinetics, uncertainty, provenance, and experimental feedback. The goal is to establish a pathway toward autonomous, digital-twin-enabled discovery of hydrogen storage materials.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01856](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01856)

## Improving Pressure Relief Valves for Liquid Hydrogen Storage Systems

To address the high-pressure risks associated with gaseous hydrogen, researchers investigated the performance of a pressure relief valve for a liquid hydrogen storage system. They used computational fluid dynamics to compare the flow characteristics of a bellows-shaped valve with a straight-shaped valve and found that the straight-shaped valve exhibited superior performance. The study aims to improve the safety of liquid hydrogen storage systems.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.3795/ksme-b.2026.50.8.435](https://doi.org/10.3795/ksme-b.2026.50.8.435)

## Comparing Conventional and Additively Manufactured Hydrogen Pressure Vessels

A study compared the life cycle assessments of conventional Type IV composite pressure vessels and additively manufactured, internally reinforced titanium alloy pressure vessels for heavy-duty vehicle applications. The researchers found that the Type IV pressure vessel exhibited approximately 16% lower energy demand and CO2 emissions compared to the titanium alloy pressure vessel. The study highlights the importance of considering the environmental impacts of hydrogen storage systems.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.3390/hydrogen7030113](https://doi.org/10.3390/hydrogen7030113)

## Exploring Al-Based Perovskite Hydrides for Hydrogen Storage

Researchers used first-principles calculations to investigate the phase stability, optoelectronic behavior, and hydrogen storage properties of Al-based metal perovskites XAlH3 (X = Ga, In, Tl). They found that these materials are thermodynamically and mechanically stable, dynamically stable, and exhibit metallic characteristics. The study suggests that these materials may be suitable for solid-state hydrogen storage applications, with estimated gravimetric hydrogen capacities ranging from 1.29 to 3.03 wt%.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1142/s0217984926501964](https://doi.org/10.1142/s0217984926501964)
