# Sci News — Crispr-Gene-Editing — 2026-09-07

## CRISPR Gene Editing: A Promising Tool for Cancer Therapy

Researchers have explored the potential of CRISPR-Cas9 in treating cancer. This gene-editing system uses the Cas9 enzyme to alter DNA sequences, inhibiting oncogenes and suppressing tumor growth. However, limitations such as off-target effects and delivery challenges restrict its clinical applications. A recent review examined the mechanisms of CRISPR-Cas9, recent advancements, and prospects for cancer management. The study also discussed strategies for precise delivery, improved targeting accuracy, and regulatory considerations for oncological applications.

## CRISPR Gene Editing for Ocular Retinal Therapy

Diabetic retinopathy and inherited retinal dystrophies cause significant vision impairment worldwide. Traditional therapies offer only symptomatic relief, but the CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing system has the potential to directly modify and correct the diseased state of the gene. A recent article described the current state of CRISPR-Cas9 for ocular retinopathies from the perspective of disease modification. The study incorporated potential barriers to design a framework to assess the future opportunities and obstacles to the CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing system in the clinic.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.2174/0129496632477238260806052732](https://doi.org/10.2174/0129496632477238260806052732)

## CRISPR Gene Editing Enhances CAR-T Cell Therapy

CAR-T cell therapy has shown significant clinical efficacy in treating haematological malignancies. However, its application is limited by challenges such as T-cell exhaustion and off-target toxicities. Recent advancements in CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing have made it possible to perform precise and combinatorial genetic editing in CAR-T cells. A review discussed the applications of CRISPR in CAR-T cell engineering, including the disruption of inhibitory immune checkpoints and the generation of universal allogeneic CAR-T cells.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.54097/x32rwy62](https://doi.org/10.54097/x32rwy62)

## CRISPR Gene Editing Reveals Immune Evasion Mechanisms in Leishmania

Leishmaniasis is a major health problem worldwide due to the parasite's ability to resist the immune response. Researchers used the CRISPR-Cas9 gene-editing system to explore the mechanisms of immune evasion used by Leishmania and identify new vaccine targets. The study successfully deleted important immune-regulation genes in Leishmania major and Leishmania donovani. The results showed a highly polarized Th1-type immune response in gene-deleted mice, indicating a potential therapeutic approach for Leishmaniasis.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.28978/nesciences.263004](https://doi.org/10.28978/nesciences.263004)

## CRISPR Gene Editing Reduces HTLV-1 Proviral Loads

Researchers demonstrated the use of CRISPR technology to excise the HTLV-1 genome and reduce proviral loads in PBMCs from HAM/TSP patients. Single treatment with CRISPR-RNP complexes resulted in excision of a 2613 bp segment of the proviral genome without detectable off-target activity. The study showed a significant reduction in proviral loads 5 days post-electroporation, indicating the potential of CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing as a therapeutic strategy to eliminate HTLV-1 DNA from infected cells.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1007/s13365-026-01333-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13365-026-01333-7)
