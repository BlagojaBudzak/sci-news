# Sci News — Sodium-Ion-Batteries — 2026-09-06

## Mo Doping Boosts Sodium-Ion Battery Capacity

Researchers have developed a new method to improve the performance of sodium-ion batteries. By doping a small amount of molybdenum into the cathode material NaMnO2, they were able to increase the battery's reversible specific capacity. The doped material showed an average working voltage of 3.0 V and an initial capacity of 183.7 mAh g-1 at 0.2 C, with a remarkable specific capacity of 138.1 mAh g-1 and distinct cycling stability of 84.7% capacity retention over 100 cycles even at 1 C.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1002/ese3.70623](https://doi.org/10.1002/ese3.70623)

## Unraveling the Interfacial Chemistry of Sodium-Ion Battery Cathodes

A comprehensive review of the interfacial instability mechanisms in sodium-based layered transition-metal oxides has been published. The review explains the fundamental thermodynamics and kinetics governing surface and interfacial behaviors, demonstrating how the coupling of bulk and interface evolution induces structural degradation and capacity decay. The authors classify stabilization strategies, including bulk-phase engineering, interfacial architecture design, and electrolyte optimization, and evaluate their efficacy in addressing specific failure modes.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1007/s41918-026-00295-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41918-026-00295-y)

## Linking Electrolytes, Electrode Compatibility, and Commercial Viability in Sodium-Ion Batteries

A critical review of sodium-ion batteries has been published, linking molecular-scale electrolyte solvation and interphase chemistry to electrode compatibility, full-cell engineering, manufacturing constraints, and commercial viability. The review compares various electrolyte types, including organic liquid, aqueous, ionic-liquid, concentrated, inorganic solid, polymer, and composite electrolytes, and evaluates the principal cathode and anode families in terms of practical voltage, reversible capacity, cycling stability, raw-material exposure, manufacturability, and end-of-life implications.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.3390/batteries12080303](https://doi.org/10.3390/batteries12080303)

## Humins-Derived Hard Carbon as a Low-Cost Anode Material for Sodium-Ion Batteries

Researchers have demonstrated the use of humins, an abundant biorefinery byproduct, as a precursor for hard carbon anodes in sodium-ion batteries. The humins-derived hard carbon showed an initial reversible capacity of 340 mAh g-1, with stable cycling performance up to 500 cycles and excellent rate capability. This study establishes humins as a promising and low-cost carbon source for high-performance SIB anodes.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1002/bte2.70144](https://doi.org/10.1002/bte2.70144)

## Sodium Compensation in Sodium-Ion Batteries: A Comprehensive Review

A comprehensive review of sodium compensation in sodium-ion batteries has been published, emphasizing the critical role of sodium compensation in improving the initial Coulombic efficiency and overall cycle life of the battery. The review provides an in-depth analysis of electrodes pretreated via Na+ pre-insertion and discusses the strategies employed to mitigate irreversible sodium loss during the initial charge/discharge cycle of the cell.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c01271](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c01271)
