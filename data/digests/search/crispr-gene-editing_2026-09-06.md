## CRISPR Gene Editing Breakthroughs in Treating Monogenic Diseases

Researchers have made significant progress in using CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technology to treat monogenic hereditary diseases. A recent review of clinical research found that CRISPR-Cas9 has achieved milestone breakthroughs in treating common monogenic disorders, with key efficacy data and safety outcomes summarized. The review also analyzed the potential of CRISPR-Cas9 in treating specific cancers and long COVID syndrome, discussing the challenges and prospects of using this technology in these areas.

## Low-Cadmium Potatoes: A Promising Solution for Food Safety

Cadmium contamination in agricultural soils poses a significant threat to food safety, particularly in potatoes. Researchers have identified low-cadmium accumulating potato varieties as a promising mitigation strategy. A review of current evidence found that stable low-cadmium phenotypes arise from coordinated regulation of soil cadmium bioavailability, rhizosphere processes, and root uptake, rather than cultivar genetics alone. Integrating low-cadmium cultivars with soil amendments and agronomic practices can provide more consistent reductions in cadmium bioavailability and tuber cadmium.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jafc.6c07783](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jafc.6c07783)

## CRISPR Gene Editing for Xenotransplantation: A Promising Approach

Severe human organ donor shortages have led to the exploration of xenotransplantation, where porcine organs are implanted into humans. However, intense rejection phenomena and molecular mismatches between species hinder this approach. Researchers have used CRISPR tools to achieve multiplex genome modifications in donor pigs, improving their molecular expression characteristics and reducing immune rejection. Early medical practices and in vivo animal experiments indicate that edited organs can evade host immune system attacks, prolonging organ survival.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.54254/2753-8818/2026.hz36113](https://doi.org/10.54254/2753-8818/2026.hz36113)

## CRISPR Gene Editing for Leishmaniasis: A New Approach to Vaccine Development

Leishmaniasis is a major health problem worldwide, with the parasite developing strategies to resist the immune response. Researchers have used CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing to explore the mechanisms of immune evasion and identify new vaccine targets. They successfully deleted important immune-regulation genes in Leishmania parasites and found that the modified parasites caused a highly polarized Th1-type immune response in infected mice. Immunization with genetically engineered antigens resulted in a significant rise in IgG antibody levels and cytotoxic T cell activity, with 80% of vaccinated mice surviving after challenge with wild-type Leishmania.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.28978/nesciences.263004](https://doi.org/10.28978/nesciences.263004)

## Advances in Hereditary Transthyretin Amyloidosis with Polyneuropathy

Hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with polyneuropathy (ATTRv-PN) is a progressive, fatal multisystem disorder with significant genotypic and geographic heterogeneity. Researchers have conducted a comprehensive review of recent advancements in the epidemiology, diagnostic workflows, and therapeutic landscape of ATTRv-PN. They found that the diagnostic paradigm has shifted towards prioritizing early genetic testing and minimally invasive biopsies, complemented by emerging serum biomarkers. Therapeutically, the landscape has been transformed by disease-modifying therapies, including gene-silencing agents and TTR stabilizers, which have demonstrated robust efficacy in halting neuropathy progression.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.1186/s13023-026-04554-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13023-026-04554-5)
