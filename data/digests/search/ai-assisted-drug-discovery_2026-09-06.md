## Evaluating the Quality of Molecular Datasets for Machine Learning

Researchers have developed a framework called MolJam to assess the quality of molecular datasets used in machine learning. They applied MolJam to 19 datasets and found widespread quality issues, including undefined stereochemistry in up to 70.72% of molecules. The study suggests that improving dataset quality may not necessarily improve machine learning performance, and that retaining molecules with incomplete stereochemical information can sometimes outperform their removal when the resulting gain in data quantity offsets the quality loss.

## The Current State of AI in Obesity Management

A review of the current state of artificial intelligence (AI) in obesity management found that direct patient-level evidence for explicitly AI-enabled obesity interventions remains limited and heterogeneous. The strongest weight or metabolic outcome evidence comes from multicomponent digital, automated, or hybrid-care programs, but this does not necessarily indicate an AI-specific therapeutic effect in obesity. AI-assisted drug discovery remains preclinical, and natural language processing of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist narratives has been explored.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1955223](https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1955223)

## A New Criterion for Evaluating Chemical Theories

Researchers have introduced the "Chemical Theory Completeness Criterion" (220), a multi-faceted evaluation of a theory's ability to describe states, state evolution, predict experimental outcomes, interpret phase boundaries, and connect across scales. This criterion aims to move beyond subjective assessments of theoretical quality and towards a quantifiable standard, particularly within the context of modern computational chemistry and AI-driven scientific discovery.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22039323](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22039323)

## AI-Driven Drug Repurposing Methodology

A review of AI-driven drug repurposing methodology focuses on selecting lead combinations of drugs for various diseases. The methodology encompasses disease characterization, integration of genomic and multi-omics data, identification of novel biomolecular targets, prediction of drug-target interactions, and prioritization of existing drugs for potential repurposing. Particular emphasis is placed on AI-based prediction of drug synergism and antagonism, where molecular targets, biological pathways, drug-response profiles, pharmacological characteristics, and drug-drug interaction data can be integrated to identify promising combinations.

## Curriculum Development and Implementation in Medical AI

A scoping review of medical AI educational programs found that the number of programs has increased rapidly but remains small, with most in the pilot stage. Learners were predominantly medical students, with small class sizes. Curriculum development mainly relied on expert experience, and learning objectives mainly focused on the understanding level. The review identified persistent gaps, including the lack of standardized curricula and the need for more research on the effectiveness of these programs.

**Read the paper:** [https://doi.org/10.2147/jmdh.s628152](https://doi.org/10.2147/jmdh.s628152)
