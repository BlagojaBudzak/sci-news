# Sci News — Seismology — 2026-09-06

## Can AI Help Predict Earthquakes?

Researchers have developed a new method to predict earthquakes using artificial intelligence and physics-based modeling. By analyzing data from slow slip events in subduction zones, they were able to identify patterns that indicate an earthquake is about to occur. In one test, their method predicted an earthquake in New Zealand's Hikurangi trench one week early. The researchers believe that their approach could potentially predict earthquakes with a lead time of up to 5-6 weeks, but more testing is needed to confirm this.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30861v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30861v1)

## New Approach to Seismic Imaging

Scientists have developed a new framework for seismic imaging called PSDWII, which takes a more physical approach to understanding seismic data. Instead of focusing on mathematical optimization, PSDWII decomposes seismic wave propagation into three physical steps: source excitation, wave interaction with heterogeneities, and wave propagation. This allows for a more unified representation of different inversion tasks, including full waveform inversion and stratigraphic imaging.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03380v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03380v1)

## Automating Seismic Data Retrieval

Researchers have created a new framework called TREMORS that uses large language models to automate seismic data retrieval. TREMORS translates natural language queries into structured workflows that can retrieve data from multiple repositories and produce high-quality, AI-ready data. This could make it easier for scientists to access and analyze seismic data, reducing the burden of routine acquisition tasks.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01777v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01777v1)

## Modeling Waves in Sea Ice

Scientists have developed a new model for wave attenuation in sea ice that takes into account the thickness of the ice. They tested this model in the Arctic Ocean and found that it performed better than an earlier model in predicting wave behavior. The new model could be useful for improving wave forecasts in areas with sea ice.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30590v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30590v1)

## Bridging Low- and High-Frequency Seismic Information

Researchers have developed a new framework for knowledge distillation that can transfer information between low- and high-frequency seismic bands. This framework, called FADKD-Net, decomposes seismic features into low- and high-frequency components and performs targeted distillation to exploit their complementary information. This could be useful for improving seismic feature learning and preserving structural continuity and fine-scale details.

**Read the paper:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00828v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00828v1)
